# E14: tiny frontier models, frozen, on BFCL

Nov 13, 2026

*Copied from the experiment's working page on 17 Nov 2026. Charts that were drawn live from data are given as tables of that data.*

## Goal

E14 measures how tiny (\~1B) models from different labs score on BFCL as they are, with no learning: each model's floor. It extends E11 (Qwen3 0.6B and 1.7B) to other model families, to compare several current 1B models.

What it tells us: each model's starting level, whether each model's BFCL handler works, which families handle multi-turn tool use at all, and the spread between them.

## Protocol

| Part | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| Exam | Official BFCL v4 through bfcl-eval 2026.3.23 (the latest release), all categories as in E11 (web search left out); multi-turn base is the main number |
| Wrapper | Each model's own handler in BFCL, unchanged; for the two newest models, a handler we write |
| Learning | None: every model frozen |
| Hardware | One NVIDIA L4 (24 GB) per model, vLLM as in E11 to E13 |
| Check | Where the BFCL leaderboard lists a model, our scores must land near it |

| Falcon3-1B | 11.1 | 10.0 | 3.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 5.4 |

- **The harness checks out:** Falcon3-1B lands on its official leaderboard entry (11.1 overall, 9.0 non-live, 2.9 live, 0.0 multi-turn, 5.8 memory; [benchmarklist](https://benchmarklist.com/models/falcon3-1b-instruct/), BFCL v4, 28 May 2026).
- **Multi-turn splits the models in two:** Falcon3-1B, Llama-3.2-1B and Gemma-3-1B solve no multi-turn task at all. The Qwen and Liquid models all solve some: Qwen3-1.7B 20% of base, the two newest models about 9%.
- **Qwen3-1.7B is still the strongest:** the newest models come close on single-turn calls (live 57 to 64 against 74) but reach half its multi-turn base score. Qwen3.5-0.8B does this at half the size.
- **Newest models, different software:** their chat formats need a newer vLLM (0.33 rather than 0.8.5), run as a separate server, and our own handlers. That vLLM raises BFCL's temperature from 0.001 to 0.01; both are still close to greedy, but the setting differs from the core models.

## Earlier work in E14: the hippocampus block

Before the redesign, E14 tried a new learner, and it collapsed. Extra LLaMA-Pro-style blocks learned after every turn from the oracle's score and were absorbed into a LoRA every 50 tasks. On Qwen3-1.7B, 300 E13 tasks, same order for every arm:

| Arm | Tasks 1 to 50 | 51 to 100 | 101 to 150 | After 150 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frozen (control) | 18 | 19 | 15 | 16 per 50; 103 of 300 overall (34%) |
| Hippo, per turn | 16 | 11 | 13 | 0 of 5, stopped at task 156 |
| Hippo, per turn and per step | 18 | 7 | 0 | 0 of 27, stopped at task 179 |

Tasks solved per block of 50. The likely causes:

- **No comparison within a task:** a wrong turn pushed down all its tokens, the shared tool-call format included; GRPO avoids this by comparing 16 attempts at the same task, where shared parts cancel.
- **No anchor:** nothing pulled the blocks back toward the original model, so small errors added up until the format broke.
- **Unchecked absorption:** each absorption copied the damage into the LoRA, which is never reset.

The per-step arm collapsed faster: its push-down on errored calls added more downward pressure. A first fix is drafted but not run: learn only from correct turns, a KL anchor to the model without the blocks, and an absorption kept only if fixed probe tasks do not get worse.

## Earlier work in E14: Step 0, Environment Tuning's checkpoints

The authors' own evaluation (T3RL) reproduces the released checkpoints within 2 points; the official BFCL harness scores every model far lower, but shows the same gain of about 26 to 28 points on base.

| Model | Base, T3RL unseen | Base, official unseen | Base, official all 200 | 4 categories, T3RL unseen | 4 categories, official unseen | Reported base / 4 categories |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 (start point) | 46.0 | 23.0 | 25.5 | 36.25 | 19.25 | none |
| EnvTuning-4B (trained on 400) | 67.0 | 45.0 | 57.0 | 61.75 | 39.75 | 69 / 63.5 |
| EnvTuning-4B-Base (trained on 100 base) | 74.0 | 51.0 | 57.0 | 58.0 | 38.25 | 75 / 60 |

BFCL multi-turn accuracy, %. Unseen: the 100 questions per category the checkpoints did not train on (T3RL split, seed 42). "All 200" includes the 100 the checkpoints trained on. EnvTuning-4B under T3RL had 19 request timeouts, scored wrong.

- **Reproduced:** under T3RL, per-category scores land within 4 points of the model cards (EnvTuning-4B-Base: 74 / 66 / 54 / 38 against 75 / 69 / 58 / 38).
- **Harness gap:** the official harness halves the base model's score (46 to 23 on base). The likely cause, not yet checked question by question, is the prompt and parser: T3RL asks for reasoning in thinking tags and its own tool-call format; the official harness uses the model's native function calling.
- **Same gain under both:** Environment Tuning adds +28 on base under T3RL (46 to 74) and +28 under the official harness (23 to 51), so the learning is real, not a harness artefact.
- **For our comparisons:** report both harnesses. The official harness stays our exam (fixed wrapper); T3RL numbers tie us to the published ones.

## Open questions

- **Gemma-3-1B:** BFCL has only a prompt-style handler for it. Keep it as a prompt-style exception, or swap it for a model with native tool calling.
- **Handlers for the newest models:** checked by hand on sample outputs (calls parsed, argument types kept, tool results passed back); no official BFCL score exists to check them against.
