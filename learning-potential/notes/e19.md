# E19: LoRA seeds and the reasoning kept

Nov 21–22, 2026

*E19 extends [E16](e16.md) (LoRA on the job) and shares the design of [E15](e15.md) to [E18](e18.md). In the paper its runs are not a separate experiment: part A's seeds join E16's results (mean and spread over three seeds), and part B is the analysis of why teaching hurts the reasoning model.*

## Goal

E15 to E18 left two weak spots in the findings the paper rests on:

1. **One seed.** Every E16 result is a single run. The large effects (LoRA lifting the zero-floor models off 0) are far beyond noise, but a reviewer at a Q1 journal will ask whether they hold over task orders, and some effects (Qwen3-1.7B judging +5 on BFCL) are borderline.
2. **An untested cause.** LoRA teaching makes Qwen3-1.7B worse (probe 51 → 39, BFCL multi-turn base 20 → 11, overall 30 → 17.5) while judging makes it better. Our explanation, that the teaching targets open with an empty think block and so teach the model to answer without reasoning, is a guess: teaching and judging also differ in whose calls are imitated (expert against own), how much is trained (every task against passed tasks) and the style of the calls.

E19 answers both with E16's code unchanged, apart from one new arm.

## Part B: the reasoning kept (arm `teach-think`)

Qwen3-1.7B, oracle teaching as in E16, with one change: each teaching target opens with a short think block instead of an empty one, and the think block is trained on with the calls.

**Where the reasoning comes from:** the base model itself (vLLM's base weights, not the adapter), shown the expected reply as a hint and asked to reason briefly about why it is the right next step, without mentioning the hint. This is STaR's rationalization (Zelikman et al., 2022). It keeps the reasoning in the model's own style and keeps the expert calls, so the only change from E16 teaching is whether the model learns to reason before calling. The reasoning is written during the replay, so later steps of a turn see the earlier steps' reasoning, as they would when the model answers for itself. Sentences that mention the hint are dropped (counted in the run's status), and a think block that hits the 512-token cap is cut at its last full sentence.

Three seeds (15, 16, 17), to compare with teaching's three. 3 runs.

| Result | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| No collapse (close to judging) | The cause is the missing reasoning: when fine-tuning a reasoning model on expert calls, keep its reasoning in the targets |
| Still collapses (close to teaching) | The cause is not the reasoning but imitating expert calls (style, amount, or overwriting a model that was already good); self-imitation does not have it |

## Protocol

Everything else as E16: rank 16, alpha 32, learning rate 1e-4, an update every 10 tasks with twice as many replayed examples, adapter hot-loaded into vLLM, snapshots every 100 tasks; probes on the 200 probe tasks ([lab/e15/probe.py](../src/lab/e15/probe.py) `--adapter`); the final adapter merged and examined on the full BFCL v4 as in E18. Same library versions as E16 (vLLM 0.8.5 for the old-stack models, the newer stack for LFM2.5 and Qwen3.5).

**Hardware:** one regular A100 per run (15 VMs, under the 16-A100 quota), reusing E16's stopped VMs where they exist. Each VM trains, then probes its own snapshots, then takes the BFCL exam, then stops ([deploy/gcp/run_e19.sh](../src/deploy/gcp/run_e19.sh)). Expected time: Qwen3-1.7B about 8 hours of training (teach-think longer, for the reasoning), Qwen3.5 about 30 hours; then about 3 hours of probes and a few hours of exam.

## Measures

As E16: gain over frozen on practice, the probe curve, and BFCL with the final adapter. For the paper, each of the six cells is reported as the mean of seeds 15 to 17 with its spread, and BFCL changes against the floor are tested per question (McNemar).

## Findings

1. **E16's main results hold over seeds.** LFM2.5, Llama and Gemma vary by 0.3–1.3 points of gain and 0.8–2.8 points on the probe. LoRA lifting Llama and Gemma off 0 on BFCL multi-turn base (to 10.3 ± 2.3 and 11.0 ± 0.5) holds in every seed. Qwen3.5's gain on practice is just as steady (+36.2 ± 0.9), but its probe and BFCL scores swing more (probe 54.5–68.5, multi-turn base 10–17, all above its floor of 8.5).
2. **Teaching makes Qwen3-1.7B unstable, rather than simply worse.** Its three seeds end the probe at 39, 61.5 and 69 and fall to 0–7% at some checkpoints on the way; E16's seed was the worst of the three. On BFCL every seed is below the floor (multi-turn base 11–18.5 against 20, overall 17.5–20.3 against 30), so the harm on the official exam is consistent.
3. **Keeping the reasoning removes the instability, and part of the harm.** With its own reasoning kept in the targets, Qwen3-1.7B learns steadily in all three seeds (probe 62.0 ± 1.3, gain +7.4 ± 0.3: its best result on our tasks) and loses far less outside multi-turn (live 68–69 and non-live 78.5–79 against 43–66 and 58–76 with teaching). But multi-turn base stays well below the floor (11.8 ± 1.0 against 20). So the empty think block explains the instability and most of the general damage, not the loss on BFCL's multi-turn tasks: imitating expert calls still costs this model there.
4. **Judging is the safe arm for the reasoning model.** Steady over seeds and the only Qwen3-1.7B arm above its floor on BFCL multi-turn base (23.0 ± 2.3, +3), with the other categories kept (overall 30.6 ± 0.5).
5. **For the paper:** the floor finding and the LoRA > retrieval > notes ranking are robust; the Qwen3-1.7B finding becomes "expert teaching destabilises a reasoning model; keeping its reasoning restores stable learning on our tasks but not on BFCL's multi-turn tasks; learning from its own verified successes is the only arm that helps it on the official exam".

## Statistical check (E20)

Tested in [E20](e20.md) (seed mean per question, sign-flip test, Holm-corrected across the 21 cell tests), finding 2 and 3 are refined:
- **Teaching, Qwen3-1.7B:** practice −5.8 is significant; the probe (+5.5) and BFCL multi-turn base (−4.3, interval −9.7 to 0.8) are not. What is certain is the damage outside multi-turn: live −16 to −31.5 and non-live −19 to −22 in every seed.
- **Reasoning kept:** significant gains on practice (+7.4) and the probe (+11.0), and a significant loss on BFCL multi-turn base (−8.2, interval −13.0 to −3.5), larger than plain teaching's. Its live and non-live losses (−11 to −13, −5 to −6) are significant but about a third of teaching's.
- **Judging:** probe +8.5 significant; BFCL +3.0 not; no significant loss in any BFCL group.
- **LFM2.5, Qwen3.5:** probe and practice gains certain; BFCL multi-turn base +3.5 and +5.7 not significant after correction. **Llama, Gemma:** all three measures significant.
