# E20: statistics and costs for E15 to E19

Nov 22, 2026

*No new runs: E20 tests every claim of [E15](e15.md) (notes), [E16](e16.md) (LoRA), [E17](e17.md) (retrieval), [E18](e18.md) (the BFCL exam) and [E19](e19.md) (seeds and the reasoning kept), and puts a cost on each method. It also keeps, in the repo, the per-question results every number rests on.*

## Goal

Before writing the paper, every claim needs a test or an interval: with 200 questions, one exam's score moves by about ±3 points by chance, and many E18 differences are that small. A Q1 reviewer will also ask what each method costs on a small device.

## What E20 keeps

| File | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| [per_question/bfcl.json](../data/per_question/bfcl.json) | Every BFCL v4 question (20 categories), solved or not, for each model's E14 floor and all 46 E15 to E19 exams |
| [per_question/probe.json](../data/per_question/probe.json) | The 200 probe tasks, for each model with nothing learnt and every snapshot (every 100 tasks) of every run |
| [per_question/practice.json](../data/per_question/practice.json) | The 1,200 practice tasks, for E15's frozen runs and all 52 learning runs |
| [stats.json](../data/stats.json) | Every test below |
| [costs.json](../data/costs.json) | Time, learned state and prompt overhead of every run |

[extract.py](../src/lab/e20/extract.py) rebuilds the per-question tables from the bucket. BFCL's score files list only failed questions, so solved = answered minus failed; each category's count is checked against BFCL's own count, and every recomputed score matches the dashboard.

## Method ([stats.py](../src/lab/e20/stats.py))

- **Paired, always:** the same questions under two conditions (learnt against the floor, a run against the model with nothing learnt, a run against the frozen model on the same practice tasks, one method against another).
- **Test:** McNemar's exact test (two-sided binomial on the questions that changed).
- **Interval:** 95% paired bootstrap over questions (10,000 resamples).
- **Many tests:** Holm's correction within each family (BFCL multi-turn base; BFCL's other groups; probe; practice; method against method; the three-seed cells). A comparison where both sides solve exactly the same questions (0 against 0) cannot reject and is left out of the family size. "Significant" below means Holm-adjusted p < 0.05.
- **Three-seed cells (E19):** the seed mean, with a t interval over seeds and a bootstrap interval over questions, tested by a paired sign-flip test of the seed mean per question.

BFCL's group scores (live, non-live) weight their subcategories; here each group's questions are pooled with equal weight, so group differences are close to, not equal to, E18's.

### 2. Single runs on BFCL multi-turn base against the floor (46 exams)

Significant after Holm: only the six LoRA teaching exams of Llama and Gemma (+8 to +12.5, every seed) and two of the three reasoning-kept exams of Qwen3-1.7B (−8.5 and −9; the third, −7, has raw p = 0.02). Nothing else, including:

- **Notes (E15):** every change within ±2 (all p > 0.4).
- **Retrieval (E17):** LFM2.5 +5.5 (raw p = 0.019) and +3.5, Qwen3.5 +4 and +5 (raw p 0.06 to 0.15), Qwen3-1.7B −3 to +3: none after correction.
- **LoRA, single runs of the middle models:** Qwen3.5 +7 and +9 (raw p 0.013 and 0.002), LFM2.5 judging +4.5 (raw p 0.035), Falcon +3 (raw p 0.031): suggestive, not significant after correction.
- **Qwen3-1.7B, LoRA teaching:** −9 in E16's seed (raw p = 0.005), but −2.5 and −1.5 in the other seeds.

### 3. Single runs on the probe against the start (52 runs)

Significant after Holm: every LoRA teaching run of LFM2.5, Qwen3.5, Llama, Gemma and Falcon (+29 to +58.5); LoRA judging of LFM2.5 (+22) and Qwen3.5 (+42.5); Qwen3-1.7B's teaching seed 17 (+18) and two reasoning-kept seeds. Not significant: every notes run (all within ±3); every retrieval run (best: LFM2.5 teaching +9.5, raw p = 0.003; Qwen3.5 +8 to +8.5, raw p 0.03); Qwen3-1.7B judging single seeds (+6.5 to +11.5, raw p 0.004 to 0.07).

### 4. Method against method (seed 15, same model and arm)

- **LoRA vs retrieval, probe:** LoRA significantly better for LFM2.5 (both arms), Qwen3.5 (both arms) and all three zero-floor models (+14.5 to +56.5).
- **LoRA vs retrieval, BFCL multi-turn base:** significantly better only for Llama (+8) and Gemma (+11). For LFM2.5 and Qwen3.5 the two are indistinguishable on the official exam (−3.5 to +4, all p > 0.16), and for Qwen3-1.7B teaching LoRA is significantly worse (−10).
- **Retrieval vs notes:** no comparison significant after correction (best: probe +8.5 to +9.5 for LFM2.5 and Qwen3.5, raw p 0.005 to 0.02).

### 5. Side effects on BFCL's other groups (significant after Holm)

- **LoRA teaching, Qwen3-1.7B:** non-live −19 to −22 and live −16 to −31.5 in every seed; memory −5.6 to −8.4.
- **Reasoning kept, Qwen3-1.7B:** non-live −5 to −6 and live −11.4 to −12.7 in every seed (about a third of teaching's loss); multi-turn −5.9 to −7.8; memory unchanged.
- **LoRA judging, Qwen3-1.7B:** no significant loss in any group, in any seed (and a significant memory gain, +8.6, in E16's seed).
- **LoRA teaching, memory:** Qwen3.5 to 0 (−4.5) in every seed, Falcon (−5.4) and LFM2.5 (−4.7) in seed 15.
- **Notes:** non-live up for LFM2.5 (+4 to +5) and Qwen3.5 judging (+9), down for Llama (−13 to −19) and Gemma teaching (−7).
- **Retrieval:** almost nothing (LFM2.5 multi-turn +3.4; Qwen3-1.7B 600-token memory +5.8).

### 6. Costs ([costs.py](../src/lab/e20/costs.py))

| Method | Learned state at task 1,200 | Grows? | Added to every prompt | Time per practice task (hardware) | Learning share of that time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Notes (E15) | 0.9 to 3.3 KB of text | No (600-token cap) | 160 to 540 tokens | 5.6 to 10.3 s; Qwen3-1.7B 76 to 79 s (L4, 3 runs sharing) | not separated (reflect and curate are generation) |
| Retrieval (E17) | log of 1.26 MB (teaching, 1,200 tasks); 0.36 to 0.57 MB (judging) | Yes, one entry per task | up to 1,200 tokens (about 2.8 examples) | 4.2 to 11.2 s (L4, 2 sharing); Qwen3-1.7B 24 s (A100, 3 sharing) | negligible (a 22M embedder on the CPU) |
| LoRA (E19, cleanest) | adapter of 3.5 MB (LFM2.5) to 70 MB (Qwen3-1.7B), float32; merged, no runtime cost | No | 0 | 7.8 s (LFM2.5) to 40 s (Gemma) and 100 s (Qwen3.5), one A100 each | 33% (Qwen3-1.7B judging) to 98% (Qwen3.5) |

- LoRA's cost is training: 70 to 98% of a task's time for teaching (judging less, as it trains only on passed tasks). Qwen3.5's trainer is slow (about 100 s a task even on an A100), its newer architecture running without fused kernels in transformers.
- LFM2.5's adapter is small (0.9M parameters) because few of its layers have the projection names LoRA targets; it learned as much as the others regardless.
- Notes and retrieval need no training but lengthen every prompt; retrieval's store grows without bound.
- Not measured: peak GPU memory during training (vLLM was given 45% of the GPU and training used the rest; no run ran out of memory after the pilot's fix).

### 7. Calling against abstaining (added 22 Nov 2026, for the paper's Figure 4)

BFCL's live and non-live groups mix two kinds of question: those that need a call, and those where no tool fits and the right answer is to make none (irrelevance and live irrelevance, 1,124 questions). The two moved in opposite directions, so section 5's group results hid what changed. They are tested apart here, with the same method (McNemar's exact test against the floor on the same questions, Holm across the figure's 92 testable cells), for every seed-15 exam and reasoning kept's seed 15: [paper/learning-potential/figures/make_figures.py](../paper/figures/make_figures.py), results in [side_effects.json](../data/side_effects.json).

- **Calling** = simple (Python, Java, JavaScript), multiple, parallel, parallel multiple, and their live versions, plus live relevance. **Abstaining** = irrelevance and live irrelevance. **Memory** as in section 5.
- **LoRA teaches every model to call a tool when none fits.** Correct abstention falls under teaching by 31 to 75 points for all six models (Qwen3-1.7B −31, LFM2.5 −31, Qwen3.5 −75, Llama −54, Falcon −74, Gemma −42) and under judging by 30 (LFM2.5) and 63 (Qwen3.5), all significant. Llama's live irrelevance, for example, falls from 68% to 7%.
- Meanwhile correct calls rise for Llama (+20) and Falcon (+33), which is why BFCL's own weighted live score rose for Llama in E18 while the pooled live group of section 5 fell.
- **Qwen3-1.7B:** teaching loses calls (−26) and abstention (−31); reasoning kept much less (−4, −20); judging neither (memory +8.6).
- **Notes shift the same balance in both directions:** LFM2.5 and Qwen3.5 call more and abstain less (Qwen3.5 teaching: calls +10, abstention −71); Llama and Gemma call less and abstain more (Llama teaching: −17, +12). Retrieval changes nothing significant.
- **Likely cause:** every practice task asks for at least one call in every turn (turns without calls were not generated), so no training target shows the model declining.

## Findings

1. **Robust:** LoRA lifts the zero-floor models (Llama, Gemma) off 0 on BFCL multi-turn base, in every seed (+10 to +11, p < 0.01 after correction). Notes and retrieval never do. This is the paper's strongest result.
2. **Robust on our tasks, not on BFCL:** LoRA's large gains for the middle models (LFM2.5 +38.5, Qwen3.5 +34.5 on the probe) are certain; on BFCL multi-turn base they shrink to +3.5 and +5.7, neither significant after correction. LoRA and retrieval cannot be told apart on BFCL for these models.
3. **Notes do nothing measurable** on the probe or BFCL multi-turn base, for any model (all p > 0.2), but change single-turn scores significantly in both directions.
4. **Retrieval's gains are suggestive only:** up to +9.5 on the probe and +5.5 on BFCL, none significant after correction, against notes or against the start.
5. **Qwen3-1.7B, revised:**
   - LoRA teaching damages its other skills in every seed (live −16 to −31.5, non-live −19 to −22). Its multi-turn base change (−4.3 over seeds) is not significant, and its probe change is unstable (−12 to +18).
   - Keeping the reasoning gives steady, significant learning on our tasks (+11 probe, +7.4 practice) and cuts the general damage to about a third. But it significantly lowers BFCL multi-turn base (−8.2, all seeds −7 to −9), more than plain teaching.
   - Judging is the only arm with no significant loss anywhere; its gain is significant on the probe (+8.5) but not on BFCL (+3.0).
6. **LoRA's main side effect is over-calling** (section 7): every model learns to call a tool when none fits, losing 31 to 75 points of correct abstention, while some call correctly more often. Training for tool use should include requests no tool can serve.
7. **For the paper:** report the three-seed means with intervals, call single-run BFCL changes under about ±5 points "not distinguishable from the floor", and describe the method ranking as holding on our tasks for all models and on BFCL only below the floor.
